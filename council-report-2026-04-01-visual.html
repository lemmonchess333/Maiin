Council Briefing

How Can We Improve the Visual Elements
on the Tropos Home Screen?

Five advisors. One verdict. A focused path forward.

April 1, 2026

⚖

Chairman's Verdict

Where the Council Agrees

  • The home screen has a hierarchy problem, not a polish problem. 11 sections competing for attention with roughly equal visual weight.
  • DayPeekCard + ActionPills + CTA cards are redundant — four of five advisors flagged this independently. All answer "what should I do next?" through different stacked UI patterns.
  • ActionPills have inverted visual weight — primary CTAs with the least visual presence.
  • Above-the-fold content is the real battleground — HealthScoreCard and the day's primary action are buried below conditional cards.

Where the Council Clashes

  • Redesign vs. incremental: B and C want to rethink from scratch (3 sections, adaptive card). E wants four scoped changes in a sprint. Peer reviews strongly favored incremental approaches.
  • Add vs. subtract: C wants more animations and features. A and B want fewer sections. Peer reviews side with subtraction.
  • Data-first vs. ship-first: A insists on scroll depth instrumentation first. E says ship four fixes now.

Blind Spots the Council Caught

  • Nobody calculated what actually fits above the fold on real devices (iPhone SE ~375×667 vs iPhone 15 ~390×844). This single measurement would resolve most prioritization debates.
  • Two audiences share one layout: daily active users need "what's next" instantly; returning/lapsed users need context.
  • Macro ring color semantics are broken: protein=teal (hydration's color), carbs=purple (brand/lifting), fat=orange (nutrition) — violates the app's own semantic color system.

The Recommendation

Do a focused hierarchy fix, not a redesign:

  1. Measure the fold — screenshot what renders above fold on iPhone SE, iPhone 15, and mid-range Android.
  2. Collapse action redundancy — when Lift/Run CTA card shows, hide matching ActionPill (2–3 hrs).
  3. Promote HealthScoreCard above the CTA cards — reorder component render, not structural refactor.

Skip: animation work, full redesign, section labels. Fix macro ring colors separately as design debt.

➤

The One Thing to Do First

Open home screen on iPhone SE viewport (375×667) with a fully active profile and screenshot what renders without scrolling. That screenshot makes every subsequent decision obvious.

Full Responses
Advisor Details
A
The Contrarian Data-first skeptic

The fatal flaw is asking how to improve visual elements without data. The home screen is too long — 11 sections, hero cards buried at positions 6–7 below conditional CTA cards. DayPeekCard competes with CTA cards (redundant IA). ActionPills have the least visual weight despite being the primary CTAs. Macro ring colors reuse semantic colors in non-semantic context (protein=teal=hydration, carbs=purple=brand, fat=orange=nutrition). Before touching CSS, instrument scroll depth.

Peer Review Highlights

Unique and valid catch on macro ring color semantics — no other advisor noticed. Scroll depth instrumentation idea is sound but risks analysis paralysis. Several peers noted this could delay action indefinitely. The color violation is worth fixing independently.

B
The First Principles Thinker Radical simplifier

The real question is why 11 sections exist. Users need one thing: what to do right now. Proposes collapsing to approximately 3 sections: a single next-action card, one headline metric with expandable stats, and one week context widget. WeekStrip + DayPeekCard solve the same problem twice. ActionPills are redundant if the next-action card always shows the right thing. "Stop polishing density. Eliminate it."

Peer Review Highlights

Correct diagnosis, overambitious prescription. Collapsing to 3 sections is a multi-sprint redesign with high risk and unclear user impact. Peers noted the "next-action card" concept is compelling but underspecified. Strongly favored as directional vision, not immediate plan.

C
The Expansionist Momentum builder

Turn the home screen into a daily momentum engine. WeekStrip should show completion/streak visualization. Collapse DayPeekCard + ActionPills + CTAs into one adaptive "Next Action" card that morphs by context. HealthScoreCard should be 2x bigger, the gravitational center. Staggered entrance animations as a daily ritual moment. "That's retention you can't buy with features."

Peer Review Highlights

Flagged by 3 reviewers for ignoring implementation cost and adding complexity to an already dense screen. The adaptive card concept overlaps with B's proposal but adds animation overhead. HealthScore promotion idea was well-received. Animation work was unanimously deprioritized.

D
The Outsider Fresh-eyes critic • Voted Strongest

11 sections is a "scroll of anxiety." Everything competes equally — when everything is important, nothing is. DayPeekCard is confusing. ActionPills + CTA cards = redundant tap targets. Hero cards are buried in the middle. Steps tile with "Connect Health" looks like a permanent error state for users without HealthKit. "Ruthless prioritization of above-the-fold content would do more than any spacing or typography tweak."

Peer Review Highlights

Voted strongest advisor by 4 of 5 reviewers. The "scroll of anxiety" framing resonated universally. Steps tile observation was a genuinely novel catch — it presents as a broken state, not a feature. Above-the-fold focus aligns with the chairman's recommendation.

E
The Executor Ship-focused pragmatist • Most Actionable

Concrete plan in order of impact-per-hour: (1) Normalize spacing to a two-tier system — 2 hrs. (2) Add section labels — 1 hr. (3) Conditionally collapse redundant ActionPills when CTA cards show — 2 hrs. (4) Add staggered fade-up to below-fold cards — 1 hr. "Ship these four, skip everything else."

Peer Review Highlights

Voted most actionable plan. Item 3 (conditional ActionPill collapse) was universally endorsed. Items 1 and 2 were seen as low-risk polish. Item 4 (animation) was deprioritized by peers — adds no functional value. Time estimates considered realistic.
