Train as one week

The question: does Train read right as one week of named sessions on days, with lifts that slide forward by themselves and runs that keep their dates, for someone who lifts, someone who runs and someone who does both? Pick an account and a state, then drive it: Next day moves today on, and every session can be finished, skipped or restored in the phone or in the state list under it. Everything the phone draws comes from one pure function, layOutWeek.

Prototype’s own choices (the grill did not decide these)
  1. A person with one sport gets one lane in the strip, not an empty second lane. The grill’s two lanes show for someone who does both.
  2. Today is an outline round the whole day column in the strip (Home outlines its one segment). The strip scrolls with the page; it is not pinned.
  3. One lift per lift day: a lift day that already has a lift done takes no other. A lift done on a rest day (“Do it today”) stays on that rest day and takes no lift day from the plan, so the lifts after it can move up.
  4. “Moved from Fri” is shown when a lift moves earlier too, because one was done ahead of plan; the grill names only the slide forward.
  5. Inside the folded past days, a lift day whose lift slid reads “Upper A moved to Thu” rather than a blank day.
  6. A missed run is drawn above today, with Move and Skip, while the past days are folded, so the fold never hides a run that needs an answer.
  7. Move offers the days from today to Sunday that hold no other run; a day with a lift is allowed. Start is offered only on a run dated today.
  8. On a rest day, today is a slim card naming the next session, with “Do it today” for the next lift. Otherwise “Do it today” sits on Still to do rows and on every lift’s own page, not on every later row.
  9. A session outside the plan (empty workout, routine, free run) takes no lift day and carries no tag; its name says what it is.
  10. An ordinary planned row carries no state word. “Next” shows only when today has nothing left to do.
  11. A run-only free runner’s week lists no days ahead (nothing is planned on them): earlier days, today’s standing card, recent runs, ways to a goal. A lifter who runs freely gets one quiet “Run when it suits you” row under today, not a second card.
  12. The weekly goal is offered as 2 to 5 runs (the app also has minutes). Once set, the count under the strip reads against it with a track. A goal has no days, so it draws no marks in the lane.
  13. A free runner’s header shows the week’s dates, since there is no plan position.
  14. The hint’s words fit the person: the grill’s sentence for someone who does both, its lift half or run half for one sport, and “Every run you save shows on its day” for a free runner.
  15. The notice slot also says when the week itself is lighter (chosen, race week, after a race), last in the grill’s order, as Q22’s “the notice above”.
  16. “Did you race?” offers Log my race and I didn’t race. “Last week didn’t go to plan” appears for a race plan when a run was missed the week before.
  17. Start opens a stand-in “in progress” screen with Finish and Leave for later; leaving puts Resume in the notice slot. A finished session comes back to the week with a toast, not a finish screen.
  18. “Start next week” is drawn (Q21) but not modelled: it shows a note.
  19. Past weeks: “‹” in the header opens last week read-only, every day open, with what carried over. “›” stays off, since next week is a later step (Q18).
  20. Edit plan opens a short sheet with Lift plan, Run plan and Usual weekly layout, the same links as Your plan.
  21. In race week the runs carry “Taper” and the one short lift carries “Lighter”.
  22. Light-theme tracks use the page grey, so an empty track reads on a white card.
  23. The race card is the plain card: no race place photograph is embedded, so nothing is fetched.
  24. A free runner’s count adds the week’s kilometres beside the number of runs; everyone else gets the one count, “3 of 7 sessions”.
  25. Every account’s week is Monday 9 to Sunday 15 November 2026; the names, numbers and exercises are examples.
