# Legal and IP audit, November 2026

What Tropos does today, checked against the law and the platform rules that
apply to a UK sole founder selling an iPhone app (and a website) to people in
the UK and the US. Read against `main` at `e2417edc` (18 November 2026) and
the open pull requests that day. File and line references are at that commit.

This is research, not legal advice. It is written for an owner who will act
on it without a lawyer, so every finding ends in the cheapest change that
removes the risk. Where only a professional can settle a point, it says so in
one line, with what is at stake.

**Read this first: this repository is public.** Anything committed here,
this file included, can be read by anyone, including the companies whose
patents and marks it discusses. In US patent law, knowing about a patent is
part of what makes infringement "wilful" and opens the door to up to three
times the damages ([35 U.S.C. 284](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/284);
[Halo v. Pulse, 579 U.S. 93 (2016)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/579/14-1513/)).
A written, element-by-element reason for believing Tropos does not infringe
is the best answer to that, and section 1.1 is written as one. Even so, the
cheapest single protection in this audit is finding 9.2: make the repository
private.

- Company, tax, ICO fee, US state laws and subscription law research: PR #3415,

## One-page summary

**Can Tropos copy Strava, Runna and Hevy?** Features, yes; expression, no.
Nobody owns the idea of a segment, a race plan built back from race day, a
props button or a rest timer. What is owned is (a) patents on specific ways
of doing some of those things, (b) the words, pictures, icons and screens
themselves, and (c) names. On all three Tropos is in good shape:

- **Patents.** Strava's segment patents need a "virtual start line" set from
  the path's direction and an "extrapolation" across it (US 9,116,922), or a
  loose and a tight match plus deleting an older overlapping segment
  (US 9,208,175). Tropos's matcher does neither: it uses a 25 m circle around
  the start and end and interpolates between recorded points. Tropos has no
  heatmap and no popularity routing, so Strava's map patents do not reach it.
  No Runna patent was found; the nearest adaptive-plan patent (Nike,
  US 11,699,523) needs critical velocity and work capacity, which Tropos does
  not compute.
- **Expression.** No copy, icon, screen or image from Strava, Runna, Hevy,
  Strong or Fitbod was found in the app. Icons are lucide (ISC), the muscle
  map is an MIT library, the art is Tropos's own. The weak spot is a
  different one: exercise photos and text that the app still pulls from a
  free GitHub catalogue whose maintainer says he does not know where the
  images came from (finding 2.2).
- **Names.** The app says "Props", not "Kudos". It uses no Strava mark
  (Strava has US filings for LIVE SEGMENTS and SUFFER SCORE; Tropos uses
  neither). Race names are used to name the race, with a not-affiliated line.
  HYROX appears nowhere in the app or the open PRs' code.

**The real risks are not in copying. They are in health data, age and the
paperwork of selling.** The ten that matter most, ranked by likelihood times

## How findings are ranked

- **Likelihood:** High means anyone who looks (a regulator, Apple, a user's
  complaint, a rival's lawyer) would find it; Medium means plausible;
  Low means unlikely at Tropos's size.
- **Impact:** High means an injunction, a five-figure cost or a blocked
  launch; Medium means a regulator's letter, forced rework or refunds;
  Low means a tidy-up.
- **Effort:** S is hours, M is a day or two, L is a week or more.

## 1. Copying competitors

### 1.1 Strava's patents (Low likelihood, High impact)

**Background.** On 30 September 2025 Strava sued Garmin in Colorado over
three patents: US 9,116,922 (segments) and US 9,297,651 and US 9,778,053
(heatmaps and routes)
([DC Rainmaker](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2025/10/strava-sues-garmin-demands-stop-selling-devices.html)).
Strava filed a voluntary dismissal without prejudice on 21 October 2025 and
the case closed on 22 October
([docket, D. Colo. 1:25-cv-03074](https://dockets.justia.com/docket/colorado/codce/1:2025cv03074/247832)).
"Without prejudice" means it can be brought again. Strava holds at least 20
US patents ([Justia, Strava, Inc.](https://patents.justia.com/assignee/strava-inc));
the ones near a Tropos feature are below.

**Where the law bites.** A US patent is infringed only by making, using or
selling in the US ([35 U.S.C. 271](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/271)),
and a method claim only when every step is carried out there
(NTP, Inc. v. Research In Motion, 418 F.3d 1282 (Fed. Cir. 2005)).
Tropos's functions run in Google's `us-central1` region
(`firebase.json:10`), so segment matching happens in the US for every user,
UK users included. Infringement needs every element of a claim (or an
equivalent of it). Missing one element is enough. In the UK only European
patents designating GB count, and software "as such" is excluded
([Patents Act 1977 s.1(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1977/37/section/1)).

#### Segments: US 9,116,922, "Defining and matching segments"

Filed 31 March 2011, so it runs to about 2031. Independent claims 1, 14, 16
and 17 ([USPTO record](https://uspto.report/patent/grant/9,116,922);
[Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9116922B2/en)). Claim 1,
element by element, against `functions/lib/segmentMatch.js`:

| Claim 1 element                                                                                                                          | Tropos                                                                                                                                                | Met?   |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| Receive a user-submitted segment definition through UI selections marking part of a first GPS track                                      | A runner makes a segment from a stretch of their own saved run (`functions/lib/segments.js:5-8`)                                                      | Yes    |
| Generate a **virtual start line**: a path through the chosen start point, the path's **orientation**, and the line set in relation to it | No line and no orientation. The start is a point with a 25 m radius (`segmentMatch.js:54`, `ENDPOINT_TOLERANCE_M`; `passes()` at `:204-223`)          | **No** |
| Determine, with a processor, an effort that matches the segment                                                                          | `matchSegment`, run by `onRunCreated`                                                                                                                 | Yes    |
| Compare the effort's GPS data with the virtual start line                                                                                | Measures distance to the start **point**                                                                                                              | **No** |
| Determine that the effort crosses the line by **extrapolating** part of its GPS data using the effort's performance metrics              | Never extrapolates. It interpolates between the two recorded points either side of the nearest approach, by position only (`interpolate`, `:226-234`) | **No** |
| Treat the effort as a match based at least in part on that crossing                                                                      | Matches on proximity, coverage of the whole line, staying on it and path length (`:1-21`, `:279-310`)                                                 | No     |

Claim 17 has the same start-line and extrapolation steps (the USPTO record,
read element by element for this audit). Claims 14 and 16 are the system and
medium versions; their full text could not be read in this session (Google
Patents did not load). Read them before relying on this table.

Dependent claims add a loose and a tight threshold (claims 10 and 11) and an
R-tree query (claim 13). Tropos has one match rule and looks segments up by
geohash cell and bounding box (`segments.js:52-57`). Dependent claims cannot
be infringed when their independent claim is not.

#### Segments: US 9,208,175, same title

Independent claims 1, 16 and 19
([RPX record](https://insight.rpxcorp.com/pat/US9208175B2)) need: a segment
defined by user-chosen start and end points; a check that it **overlaps an
existing segment from a different effort**; **deleting the older one**; and
matching with "a first threshold … for a loose match or a second threshold …
for a tight match", where a tight match crosses "a start line and a finish
line". Tropos does not check for overlapping segments
(`createSegment` has no such step; `grep -n overlap functions/lib/segments.js`
finds nothing), deletes nothing on creation, and has one match rule with no
lines. Not met.

#### Segment validation: US 9,709,408 and EP 2 825 850

The US claim revises a segment's points to centroids computed from **many
people's matching efforts** ([RPX record](https://insight.rpxcorp.com/pat/US9709408B2)).
Tropos never changes a segment after it is made, and never pools other
people's traces. The European application with the same title, EP 2 825 850
(priority 12 March 2012), designates GB
([EPO](https://data.epo.org/publication-server/rest/v1.2/patents/EP2825850NWA1/document.html)).
Its granted claims and whether it is still renewed in the UK were not read:
check the [UK register](https://www.ipo.gov.uk/p-ipsum.htm) by number. Its
title is the same idea, which Tropos does not use.

#### Live segments: US 9,291,713 (and US 9,804,269)

Claim 1 needs a user-set "window of concurrency" and **two users on the same
segment at the same time** ([RPX record](https://insight.rpxcorp.com/patent/US9291713B2)).
Tropos's nearest feature, the ghost on a re-run route
(`src/components/run/GhostDeltaChip.tsx:15-24`), compares a runner with
their own earlier recording, not with another live user. Not met. The
continuation US 9,804,269 could not be read (the USPTO copy had no text).
Read its claims before building anything that times a live run against a
segment.

#### Comparing two tracks: US 9,664,518

Priority 27 August 2010, so it runs to about 2031. Claim 1 aligns two tracks
of the same route by removing redundant points to make **anchor series**,
**resampling** paths between anchors, **scoring** the alignment and mapping
it back, then shows the comparison against distance
([USPTO record](https://uspto.report/patent/grant/9664518)). The ghost finds
the nearest point on the earlier route by projection and reads the time the
earlier run took to that distance (`src/lib/gps.ts:82-141`,
`routeProgress`, `routeTimeAtDistance`). No anchors, resampling or alignment
scoring. Not met literally. This is the closest of the patents in idea, so
keep the ghost as simple as it is.

#### Heatmaps and routes: US 9,297,651, 9,778,053, 10,240,939, 12,584,756

These claim maps built by aggregating many users' activities, and routes
suggested from that data between endpoints the user gives (claim 1 of
9,297,651 includes "determining one or more suggested routes between a user
input first endpoint and a user input second endpoint", quoted by
[DC Rainmaker](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2025/10/strava-sues-garmin-demands-stop-selling-devices.html)),
or a generative model trained on recorded activities (12,584,756). Tropos has
no heatmap (the only "heat map" is the muscle map,
`src/components/analytics/MuscleHeatMap.tsx`), never aggregates other
people's routes, and its planner draws straight lines between the points a
person taps, with no routing service (`src/lib/routePlanner.ts:1-12`). Road
routing on Mapbox was removed on 1 November 2026
(`docs/app-store/listing.md:412-422`). Not met.

#### Others on Strava's list

GPS data repair (9,534,908; 10,323,948), grade-adjusted pace from aggregate
data (11,623,121), clustering similar activities (10,515,101) and top venues
(10,228,258): Tropos has no feature built on other people's pooled data, and
no grade-adjusted pace (no match for "grade adjusted" in `src/` or
`functions/lib/`). Kudos, Relative Effort, Beacon, Best Efforts and privacy
zones: no Strava patent found on the first page of its list (page 2 was not
read). Tropos has no Beacon (live location sharing) and no Relative Effort.
Its training load card is a fitness-fatigue model
(`src/lib/trainingLoad.ts:1-20`) from Banister's 1975 work, long in the
public domain.

#### What this means

No Tropos feature has every element of any Strava claim read here. The
residual risk is the doctrine of equivalents, the claims not read (9,116,922
claims 14 and 16, 9,804,269, page 2 of the list, EP 2 825 850's granted
claims) and a claim letter as Tropos grows. Strava sued Garmin, a partner
with billions in revenue; there is no sign it pursues small apps.

**Fix (S): keep these out of the code, and re-read this section before
adding any of them.** Put the list in a comment at the top of
`functions/lib/segmentMatch.js`, so the next change to the matcher meets it:

- no start or finish line set from the path's direction, and no
  extrapolating a trace across one;
- no pair of loose and tight match thresholds;
- no merging or deleting a segment because a newer one overlaps it;
- no revising a segment from other people's efforts;
- no live comparison between two people on a segment at once;
- no heatmap, and no routes suggested from other people's activity;
- no anchor-and-resample alignment of two tracks.

Only a US patent attorney can give an opinion of non-infringement that
carries weight in court; that costs thousands of dollars and is worth it
only if Strava writes.

### 1.2 Runna and adaptive plans (Low, Medium)

No patent or application by Runna (Fitness Plus Ltd) was found. The nearest
granted patent is Nike's US 11,699,523, "Adaptive athletic activity
prescription systems" (priority 15 March 2016, to about 2037;
[Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11699523)). Its only
independent claim needs a motion sensor, a benchmark run, an experience
class with assigned constants, **critical velocity and finite work
capacity** computed from the run, adjusting the plan during the current run,
and two or more options ranked by how likely the person is to finish each.
Tropos sets paces from a race time with Daniels' VDOT (`src/lib/racePace.ts:38-43`),
computes no critical velocity or work capacity, and offers no ranked options.
Not met. Training methods, Daniels' tables, base-build-taper and the like are
ideas and published science, which nobody owns.

**Fix:** none. Do not add critical-speed modelling without re-reading this
claim.

### 1.5 Tropos's own name (Medium, Medium)

Nobody has registered TROPOS for fitness. In the US, TROPOS is registered by
Hitachi Energy for radio networking equipment and software in class 9
([record](https://markinton.com/trademark/tropos-76596451)), and CloudVirga
has a pending application for mortgage software in classes 9, 36 and 42,
published for opposition on 9 June 2026
([record](https://markinton.com/trademark/tropos-99196721)). Both are far
from fitness, so using the name is low risk, but the CloudVirga filing may
be cited against a Tropos application in class 9. The UK register was not
searched. Tropos owns no registration, so a later filer could make life hard.

**Fix (S):** search the [UK register](https://trademarks.ipo.gov.uk/ipo-tmtext)
for TROPOS in classes 9, 41 and 42, then file a UK application in those
classes ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/how-to-register-a-trade-mark); a few
hundred pounds). File in the company's name once it exists (9.1). A US
filing can wait for US traction.

## 2. Content provenance

### 2.1 Exercise and run-form art (Low, Low)

**Today.** All 152 exercise sets and the run-form guide are drawn with
Google's Gemini through `scripts/art/gemini-image.py`, assembled and
reviewed in the repo (`docs/exercise-art/README.md`). Some source folders
keep reference pictures used to check equipment shape, among them a
manufacturer's product photograph
(`docs/exercise-art/sources/face-pulls/2026-10-15/reference/lifemaxx-lmx2315-manufacturer-reference.png`)
and a screenshot the owner supplied of a calf-raise machine.

**Rule.** Google does not claim ownership of what Gemini generates
([Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)). In the UK a
computer-generated work is protected, with the person who made the
arrangements as author
([CDPA 1988 s.9(3)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/9);
50 years under [s.12(7)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/12)),
though the government's March 2026 report said that section could go
([HSF Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/ip/2026-03/uk-government-report-on-copyright-and-ai-concludes-more-evidence-is-needed-although-s9-3-cdpa-could-go)).
In the US, art with no human author has no copyright
([US Copyright Office, AI](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/)). Copying a
manufacturer's photograph into a public repository is a reproduction of it.

**Risk.** Tropos is free to use its art. A rival could copy it in the US
without infringing. The manufacturer's photo in a public repo is a small,
technical infringement.

**Fix (S):** delete third-party reference photographs from the repository
(keep a link to where they came from instead), or make the repository
private (9.2).

### 2.2 Exercise photos and instructions from free-exercise-db (Medium, Medium)

**Today.** The form guide fetches `exercises.json` and photographs from
`yuhonas/free-exercise-db` on GitHub at a pinned commit
(`src/lib/exerciseDemo.ts:228-241`). They are shown when Tropos has no drawn
guide or no authored text for the exercise name: the photos as "reference
photos", the catalogue's instructions as the steps
(`src/lib/exerciseDemo.ts:384-410`; `src/components/ExerciseFormContent.tsx:177, 194`).
The Privacy Policy names GitHub for these images (`src/pages/PrivacyPolicy.tsx:549-551`).

**Rule.** The repository is marked Unlicense, but its maintainer says of the
images: "I actually have no idea where the images are from or if they are
royalty free so usage would be at your own risk"
([issue #2](https://github.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db/issues/2)). The data
came from `wrkout/exercises.json`, which names no source either
([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db/main/README.md)).
A licence from someone who does not own the work gives no protection.

**Risk.** Medium likelihood that the photos and text are someone's
commercial work; a takedown or a claim for licence fees would follow. It is
shown inside a paid app.

**Fix (S):** delete the remote fetch and the `reference-photos` path. Every
catalogue exercise now has released drawn art (`docs/exercise-art/README.md`,
20 October checkpoint), so the fallback only fills gaps for odd names and
custom exercises, where the muscle diagram already stands in. Remove the
GitHub line from the Privacy Policy with it.

### 2.3 Welcome and plan photographs (Low, Low)

(`plan-no-race.webp`) is a free Unsplash photo of runners

## 3. Privacy and data

### 3.1 Health data has no separate explicit consent (High, Medium)

**Today.** The only agreement is one line under the sign-up buttons: "By
continuing, you agree to the Terms and Privacy Policy" (`src/pages/Login.tsx:567-577`).
Setup then asks for weight, height, year of birth, sex, injuries and goals,
with a "Why these details?" note (`src/pages/Onboarding.tsx:2160-2172`), and
the app later stores weigh-ins, the food diary, heart-rate summaries from
runs and injury limits. The Privacy Policy gives one legal basis for
everything, "your consent (provided during account creation …) and for the
performance of our service" (`src/pages/PrivacyPolicy.tsx:359-364`), and
names no Article 9 condition.

**Rule.** Health data is special category data. Processing it needs a lawful
basis and one of the Article 9 conditions
([UK GDPR Art. 9](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/9)); for
a consumer app that is explicit consent, which must be separate from the
terms, specific and an affirmative act, and as easy to withdraw as to give
([Art. 7](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/7);
[ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/special-category-data/what-are-the-rules-on-special-category-data/)).
Agreeing to the Terms and the policy by continuing is not explicit consent to
anything.

**Risk.** High likelihood (anyone who reads the sign-up screen sees it),
Medium impact: an ICO complaint ends in a reprimand and an order to fix
rather than a fine at Tropos's size, but every account made before the fix
would sit on no valid condition.

**Fix (M):** one screen in setup, before the body details, with its own
button:

> **Your health details.** Tropos stores your weight, height, food, injuries
> and heart rate from your runs to build your plan and your calorie target.
> This is health data. We use it only for your plan and never for ads.
> You can delete it, or your account, at any time.
> [I agree] [Why we ask]

Store the answer with a date (`healthDataConsentAt`) and send an account that
has not agreed back to the screen. Withdrawal is Delete account, which the
policy already offers; say so on the screen. Change the policy's legal-basis
line to name Article 9(2)(a) explicit consent for health data, contract for
the account, and legitimate interests for security and moderation.

### 3.3 Health figures in Google Analytics (High, Medium)

**Today.** Chart taps send the value tapped: the macro share
(`src/components/analytics/MacroDistribution.tsx:147-151`), the performance

(`History.tsx:1687-1689`). The listing declares that health and fitness

**Rule.** In the UK this is special category data shared with a third party

Washington's My Health My Data Act needs a separate consent before

([RCW 19.373](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373)). The

**Risk.** High likelihood (the listing says it), Medium impact; the
Washington law is the one with private claims.

**Fix (S):** remove the `value` param from those four calls (the event still
says which chart was tapped), and check the other analytics files for any

### 3.4 Analytics and the device storage rules (Medium, Low)

**Today.** Google Analytics runs on the web (`src/lib/analyticsProvider.ts:116-131`)

an exception applies ([PECR reg. 6](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/6)).
Since 5 February 2026 there is one for analytics used "solely to collect
statistics on how a service or website is used with a view to improving it",
provided the person gets clear information and a simple, free way to object
([bratby.law](https://bratby.law/data-use-and-access-act-2025-commencement/);
[Blake Morgan](https://www.blakemorgan.co.uk/data-use-and-access-act-2025-cookies/)).

**Fix (S):** a "Share usage analytics" switch in Settings → Social & privacy
(on by default), wired to `setEnabled` on iPhone and to not initialising
analytics on the web; a sentence in the policy saying what it collects and
how to turn it off; and in the Google Analytics console, turn off data
sharing with Google products and keep Google Signals off (already a
listing condition, `docs/app-store/listing.md:181-185`), so the use stays
"solely" Tropos's statistics.

**Rule.** The ICO's Age Appropriate Design Code covers anyone under 18 on a

**Fix (S):** make the minimum 18: setup's year check, the Terms, the policy,
and the App Store rating (18+). This is the business plan's decision 6 too

children's assessments (7.1), because a typed year is not age assurance.

## 5. Consumer and subscription law

### 5.1 Pro bought in the App Store (Low today, Medium from January 2027)

**Today.** The paywall leads with what is charged after the trial and says it
renews and where to cancel (`src/lib/proPlans.ts:209-253`,
`src/components/paywall/RenewalDisclosure.tsx`); the Terms say the same
(`src/pages/TermsOfService.tsx:61-95`); "Save X%" is worked out from the
store's own prices (`proPlans.ts:183`); no countdowns or "offer ends" copy
was found. Tropos emails and notifies every trial two days before the last
day to cancel (`functions/lib/trialReminder.js:1-21`). PRs #3399 and #3406
fix the two paywall risks App Review would catch.

**Rule.** UK: the Consumer Contracts Regulations give 14 days to cancel
digital content until supply starts with express consent and an
acknowledgement ([reg. 37](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/37));
Apple's purchase sheet carries that. The DMCC Act's subscription rules
([Part 4, Chapter 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/part/4/chapter/2))
are due in January 2027
([Lewis Silkin, 15 Sep 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/09/15/key-takeaways-the-subscription-shake-up-are-you-ready-for-january-2027-102o14n);
[DWF, Oct 2026](https://dwfgroup.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/10/dmcca-subscription-contracts-regime-preparing-for-the-new-restrictions)):
key information before sign-up, reminders before a trial converts and at
set intervals, cooling-off periods, and an easy way out, with CMA fines of up
to 10% of turnover. US: [ROSCA](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/8403)
(clear terms, express consent, simple cancellation); the FTC's click-to-cancel
rule was struck down in July 2025 and rulemaking restarted in March 2026
(PR #3415, section 7.6); California's auto-renewal law
([BPC 17602](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=17602)).

**Risk.** In the App Store, Apple runs consent, cancellation and refunds.
Whether Tropos or Apple is the "trader" for the DMCC duties depends on
whether Apple sells as Tropos's agent or in its own name in the UK
(Schedule 2 and Exhibit A of the Apple Developer Program License Agreement).
That is unconfirmed, and only a solicitor can settle it; what is at stake is
whether Tropos must send its own six-monthly reminders and refund in the
cooling-off periods, which it cannot do for App Store purchases.

**Fix:** nothing before launch. Before January 2027, read the final
regulations and the CMA's guidance for how they treat app store
subscriptions; extend the trial reminder to a yearly renewal reminder if they
put the duty on the developer.

### 5.2 The website checkout (High if switched on, Medium)

**Today.** Built but off until `REVENUECAT_WEB_PURCHASE_TOKEN` is set
(`docs/iap/revenuecat-setup.md:200-213`). On RevenueCat Billing over
Tropos's own Stripe account, **Tropos is the seller of record**, to the UK,
the EU and the US (App1 Q77). The web's renewal line reads "Renews
automatically until cancelled. Cancel any time." (`src/lib/proPlans.ts:236`).
RevenueCat's checkout can add a required terms checkbox for subscriptions,
and is silent on cancellation-right wording
([RevenueCat docs](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/web/web-billing/checkout)).

**What selling directly needs:**

- **UK:** the pre-contract information in
  [CCR Schedule 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/schedule/2)
  (including Tropos's geographic address), a box where the buyer asks for Pro
  to start at once and acknowledges losing the 14-day right, and a
  confirmation on a durable medium. Without them the cancellation period runs
  up to 12 months and the buyer need not pay for what was supplied
  ([reg. 31](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/31),
  [reg. 37(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/37)).
- **EU:** the same, plus a "withdraw from contract here" function since
  19 June 2026 (PR #3415, section 7.6), and EU VAT through the non-Union One
  Stop Shop **from the first sale**, since no threshold applies to a seller
  outside the EU.
- **US:** ROSCA and California's law: the renewal terms next to the button,
  a consent the buyer gives to the renewal itself, an acknowledgement email
  with how to cancel, online cancellation, and sales tax state by state once
  each threshold is passed.
- **Records:** California wants consent records kept for three years.

**Fix (S): keep the web checkout off at launch.** The App Store sells Pro
everywhere the app is. When the web opens, either sell to UK buyers only
(turn on RevenueCat's terms checkbox, add the start-now acknowledgement to the
Terms and the checkout page, send a confirmation email from the webhook
through Resend with the cancellation terms), or use a merchant of record
(RevenueCat's Paddle engine), which becomes the seller and takes VAT,
sales tax and buyer support. The owner finds Paddle's fee too high
(App1 row); at Tropos's web volume the fee is smaller than the cost of EU VAT
returns and US registrations done by hand.

## 6. Advertising and health claims

### 6.1 The Pro demo shows placeholder numbers (Medium, Low)

**Today.** The scan demo on the Pro offer and the Upgrade page shows a result
of 720 kcal for the rigatoni photo (`src/components/paywall/ScanDemo.tsx:68-82`),
and its README says "Until the owner's scan of it is in, the values there are
placeholders" (`src/assets/pro-demo/README.md`).

**Rule.** An ad must not mislead about what the product does
([CAP Code 3.1](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html));
Apple 2.3 says the same of the App Store page, and the demo is the paywall.

**Fix (S):** scan the photo on a Pro account and put the scanner's real
answer in `SCAN_DEMO_RESULT`, as the README says.

### 6.2 Injury wording (Low, Medium)

**Today.** Injury swaps write the rationale into the exercise's note, which
the plan and the saved workout show (`src/features/program/matchTemplate.ts:290`;
`src/components/program/ExerciseRowSummary.tsx:126-129`;
`src/pages/WorkoutDetail.tsx:213-215`). One calls a lift "clinically knee-friendlier than leg press"
(`src/features/program/injurySubstitutions.ts:564`); others call a lift
"spine-safe" (`:390`); setup says a lift "stays with no safe swap"
(`src/components/onboarding/InjuryChangesList.tsx:56`).

**Rule.** "Clinically" claims need clinical evidence for that claim
([CAP Code 12.1](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/12.html)).
Calling a swap "safe" for an injury is a promise about a person's body that
Tropos cannot keep, and it reads as advice on treatment, the line between a
fitness app and a medical device (6.5).

**Fix (S):** reword the rationales to say what changes, not that it is safe
or clinical ("Lighter on the knee: one leg at a time"), and change "no safe
swap" to "no swap". Add the warning in 6.3 under the injury choices.

### 6.3 No exercise warning in the app (Medium, Medium)

**Today.** The only health warning is in the Terms and the Privacy Policy
(`src/pages/TermsOfService.tsx:160-171`; `PrivacyPolicy.tsx:619-626`). Setup
builds lifting and running plans and a calorie target with no warning on
screen.

**Rule.** UK law does not let a trader exclude liability for death or injury
caused by negligence, and agreeing to a term does not mean accepting the risk
([CRA 2015 s.65](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/65)).
What a warning does is make a claim harder: it shows reasonable care and
supports contributory negligence. A warning nobody sees does neither.

**Fix (S):** one line on setup's last screen and under the injury choices:

> Check with a doctor before you start if you have a health condition, an
> injury or are pregnant. Stop and get advice if you feel pain, dizziness or
> chest pain.

### 6.4 Weight-loss targets at any weight (Medium, Medium)

**Today.** A target weight below the current one sets a deficit
(`src/lib/goalWeightPlan.ts`), floored at 1,200 kcal and 0.75 kg a week
(PR #3396, 1.4.1). Nothing checks body mass index: someone already
underweight can set a lower target and get a deficit. No match for "BMI" or
"underweight" anywhere in `src/` or `functions/lib/`.

**Rule.** CAP's weight-control rules bar weight-loss marketing to people for
whom it would be harmful, naming a BMI under 18.5
([CAP Code 13.3](https://asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/13.html)).
Ofcom names eating-disorder content as primary priority content for children
([OSA s.61](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/61)), and a
calorie app that helps an underweight person lose more is the facts of a
negligence claim.

**Fix (S):** when height and weight put BMI under 18.5, or the target weight
would, don't offer a deficit: set maintenance and say why in one line, with a
link to [Beat](https://www.beateatingdisorders.org.uk/) in the UK.

### 6.5 Medical device status (Low, High)

Tropos tracks training and food, estimates calories with standard formulas,
and says readiness in words from the person's own sleep and heart rate. None
of that is diagnosis, treatment or monitoring of a disease, which is what
makes software a medical device in the UK
([MHRA](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/medical-devices-software-applications-apps))
or the US, where the FDA widened its wellness policy in January 2026
([Covington](https://cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/fda-issues-revised-guidance-on-general-wellness-products)).
App Store Connect's medical-device question is answered No (PR #3396). The
words that would change this are "treat", "rehab", "recover from injury",
"diagnose" and claims about conditions; none was found in shown copy. Keep it
that way, and keep 6.2's fix.

### 6.6 The listing (Pass)

The description claims estimates "from your numbers", nothing "unlimited",
no "science-backed", no weight-loss amounts, and says Tropos is not a medical
device (`docs/app-store/listing.md:66-116, 285`). The submission pack
(PR #3418) keeps that. Pass.

## 7. Social features and the Online Safety Act

### 7.1 Assessments (Medium, Medium)

**Rule.** Posts, comments, Spaces, Circles, segments' names and profile
photos make Tropos a user-to-user service, with no exemption for small
services. A new service must complete its illegal content risk assessment and
its children's access assessment **within three months of the day it
starts** ([OSA Schedule 3, para. 3(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/schedule/3)),
and a children's risk assessment within three months of concluding children
are likely to use it. A typed age is not "highly effective" age assurance, so
even at 18+ the children's assessment is likely needed (PR #3415, section 7.3).
Ofcom checks pre-launch assessments now
([Lewis Silkin, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/10/22/ofcom-investigation-puts-pre-launch-risk-assessments-in-the-spotlight-102o663)).

**Fix (M):** do both assessments in the first month with Ofcom's free
online tools, keep the records, and redo them before a significant change
(public segment boards, direct messages, open groups).

### 7.2 Reporting and terms (Medium, Low)

**Today.** Report reasons: harassment (with "Encouraging self-harm"), spam,
inappropriate content, impersonation, other (`src/lib/reportCategories.ts:20-60`).
Every report is stored and emailed, and the Terms promise review within 24
hours (`src/pages/TermsOfService.tsx:149-152`). A restricted account sees
"Contact support". Segment names cannot be reported (PR #3396, 1.2).

**Rule.** The Act asks for easy reporting of illegal content and content
harmful to children, a complaints route that covers appeals against removal
or restriction, and terms that say how users are protected.

**Fix (S):** add sub-reasons "Promotes eating disorders", "Child safety",
"Illegal content" and "Copyright"; add a sentence to the Terms that a person
whose post was removed or whose account was restricted can ask for a review
at support@troposfit.com; name eating-disorder promotion in the Terms' list
of what is not allowed (`TermsOfService.tsx:140-148`).

### 7.3 Defamation and harassment (Low, Low)

The [Defamation Act 2013 s.5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2013/26/section/5)
defence works for an operator who follows the notice procedure or takes the
post down. Removing on a credible complaint, as the moderation page can,
is enough at Tropos's size. The word filter
(`src/lib/profanityFilter.ts`, `functions/profanityFilter.js`) catches slurs
and profanity, not defamation, which is expected.

## 8. Accessibility (Low, Low)

The [Equality Act 2010 s.29](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/section/29)
requires reasonable adjustments in services, without naming a standard.
Tropos tests large text at 2× on every main screen
(`e2e/screenshots/largeText.capture.spec.ts`) and keeps accessibility floors
in `DESIGN_GUIDE.md` §10. The EU's Accessibility Act exempts
micro-enterprises providing services. No fix beyond keeping those floors.

## 9. Company and admin

### 9.1 Sole trader (Medium, High)

**Today.** The Terms and the policy name Myles Kennedy as the trader and
controller (`src/pages/TermsOfService.tsx:213-227`;
`src/pages/PrivacyPolicy.tsx:48-61`); the listing's copyright line is his
name (`docs/app-store/listing.md:150`).

**Risk.** Everything above (an injury claim, a regulator's order, a
chargeback wave, a patent letter) is the owner's personal debt. Apple may
also want a legal entity for an app holding health data (section 4).

**Fix (M):** incorporate a private limited company (£100 online,
[GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation)), then: Apple
organisation enrolment (needs a D-U-N-S number), the ICO fee, the trade mark
filing and the Terms and policy names in the company's name. PR #3415,
section 7.1, has the steps. Public liability and professional indemnity
insurance for a fitness app is worth a quote at the same time.

### 9.2 The public repository (Medium, Medium)

**Today.** `lemmonchess333/Maiin` is public and has no licence
(`gh api repos/lemmonchess333/Maiin`: `"visibility": "public"`,
`"license": null`). It holds the code, the plan file, the business plan
(PR #3415), the security audit (PR #3411), licensed photographs as files,
a manufacturer's photograph (2.1) and this patent analysis.

**Risk.** No licence means nobody may legally reuse the code, but anyone can
read it, and copying is hard to prove. The business plan and security audit
help competitors and attackers. Section 1.1 is evidence of knowing Strava's
patents (see the top of this file).

**Fix (S):** make the repository private. Two costs to weigh: GitHub Pages
from a private repository needs a paid plan, and private repositories get a
monthly Actions allowance, which the five CI variants may exceed. Firebase
Hosting is already the product surface. If it must stay public, at least add
an "All rights reserved" `LICENSE`, and keep `docs/business/`,
`docs/agents/security-audit-*` and `docs/legal/` in a private repository.

### 9.3 Business name and address (Medium, Low)

**Rule.** A business trading under a name other than its owner's must give
the owner's name and a UK address for service on its website, invoices and
emails ([Companies Act 2006 s.1202](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/1202)),
and an online service must show a geographic address
([E-Commerce Regulations 2002 reg. 6](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2002/2013/regulation/6)).
The CCR's pre-contract information needs one too (5.2).

**Today.** The Terms, policy and support page give a name and an email, no
address.

**Fix (S):** a registered office or address service (so the home address is
not published), shown on `/support`, `/terms` and `/privacy`. Incorporating
gives the registered office at the same time.

### 9.5 VAT (Low now, Medium with the web checkout)

Apple collects and remits VAT on App Store sales to customers. Whether App
Store proceeds count towards Tropos's £90,000 UK registration threshold
depends on how HMRC treats Apple's role; PR #3415 (section 7.7) sends that
question to an accountant. At stake: VAT on revenue above the threshold if
the answer is yes. The web checkout is where VAT bites from the first sale
(5.2).

### 9.6 Encryption export (Pass)

`ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption` is false (`ios/App/App/Info.plist:68-69`).
The app uses HTTPS and the operating system's own AES-GCM through WebCrypto
for progress photos (`src/lib/progressPhotoCrypto.ts:36-42`): encryption
provided by Apple's own operating system, which App Store Connect's export
questions treat as exempt. Keep the answer in step if a crypto library is
ever bundled.

### 9.7 EU storefronts (Medium, Medium)

If the app is offered in EU App Stores: GDPR Article 27 asks a non-EU
controller of health data to appoint an EU representative (the "occasional
processing" exemption does not fit), the DSA trader details are published,
and the EU withdrawal button applies to web sales. **Fix (S):** release in
the UK and US storefronts only at launch (App Store Connect → Pricing and
Availability), as PR #3415 also suggests.
