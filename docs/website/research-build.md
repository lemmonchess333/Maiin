# troposfit.com: what six fitness apps' sites do well

Read on 18 November 2026, before designing troposfit.com
([build.md](./build.md)). Each site was read for its structure, its
words and how it shows the app. What Tropos takes from each, and what it
leaves, is at the end.

## Runna

[runna.com](https://www.runna.com/en-gb).

- **One promise, said plainly.** "Real coaching from real runners", then
  what that means: plans from a faster 5K to a first marathon.
- **The app doing a real thing, step by step.** Its race-time estimator is
  three phone screens in a row: choose level and distance, enter a recent
  5K time, see "3:40 to 3:45" for a marathon in 10 weeks. A visitor sees
  what they would get before installing.
- **A plan finder by distance** (5K, 10K, half, marathon, ultra), each a
  page of its own: someone searching for a half marathon plan lands on
  one.
- **How it works in four steps**: download, set your goal, start
  training, achieve more.
- **Price beside the button**, with the free first week said every time.
- Less good: duplicated coach bios, and a hidden price table with blank
  cells.

## Strava

[strava.com](https://www.strava.com/?v3=true) and its
[subscription page](https://www.strava.com/subscribe).

- **The promise is the community**: "Community-Powered Motivation", and
  "Join for the tracking, stay for the community."
- **Three app screens, each with a verb**: "Start by sweating", "Get better
  by analysis", "Dive into details on desktop". Each is one real screen
  (an activity summary, an analysis, a training log) and two sentences.
- **The subscription page explains the trial as a timeline** (Today, 2
  days before, In 30 days) and compares Free and Subscription feature by
  feature.
- Less good: the home page repeats its own sections, and its main way in
  is a web sign-up, which troposfit.com does not have (App1 Q52).

## Hevy

[hevyapp.com](https://www.hevyapp.com/) and its
[feature pages](https://www.hevyapp.com/features/gym-performance/).

- **A three-verb headline**: "Log Workouts, Get Stronger, Stay
  Motivated", and a one-line subhead that says what it is and where it
  runs.
- **Each section is one job with one screen**: log workouts, measure
  progress, join the community. Each has a heading, a sentence and a list
  of what the screen does.
- **The badge at the top and again at the close.**
- **Feature pages of their own** under /features/, for search.
- **A press page in the footer** with the other company links.
- Less good: ratings, user counts and media logos lead, which a new app
  cannot honestly show.

## Ladder

[joinladder.com](https://www.joinladder.com/) and its
[site index](https://www.joinladder.com/llms.txt).

- **One way on, everywhere**: every button goes to the same plan quiz.
- **"Know exactly what workout to do, every day"** over a phone showing a
  week's calendar: the promise is the week, and the picture is the week.
- **A short FAQ that answers the reasons not to install**: do I need a
  card, can I switch, home or gym.
- Less good: a celebrity hero, a grid of coach posters, and inconsistent
  capitals ("eVERY").

## Nike Run Club

[nike.com/gb/nrc-app](https://www.nike.com/gb/nrc-app).

- **Every section a heading, a paragraph, a picture and one action**:
  "We keep track of the details", "Welcome to your starting line",
  "Celebrate achievements".
- **A QR code for a desktop visitor**, who cannot install from the page.
- **Achievements are shown as the reward screens themselves.**
- Less good: no store badges, and the images carry no description for a
  screen reader ("Nike Run Club App").

## MacroFactor

[macrofactor.com](https://macrofactor.com/macrofactor/).

- **The method explained plainly**: the target comes from what you log
  and how your weight moves, and it is "adherence-neutral": a high day is
  not punished. A visitor learns why the number is right.
- **Candid answers** in its FAQ, including what it does not do (no free
  tier, no wearable data for the target).
- **Values said as values**: no ads, privacy first.
- **A press kit in the footer.**
- Less good: four badge placements on one page.

## What troposfit.com takes

1. **One promise, the welcome's own**: "Your training and food, planned
   together". It is what the app says on its first screen (DESIGN_GUIDE
   §14p), so the site and the app open the same way.
2. **The app doing real things** (Runna, Strava, Hevy, Nike): real screens
   from the capture rig, on demo accounts, each beside the sentence that
   says what it does, with a number in it.
3. **A page per part** (Hevy, Runna): Lifting, Running, Food and Social,
   each with its own title and preview, so a search for a lifting plan or
   a half marathon plan can land on one.
4. **The week as the picture** (Ladder): Home's second section is a day,
   its third the week.
5. **The method in plain words** (MacroFactor): how the target is set,
   how lifts move up, how a league is scored.
6. **Short answers to the reasons not to install** (Ladder, MacroFactor),
   on the Pro page: can I buy it here, how do I cancel, what happens if
   Pro ends.
7. **A press kit and an About page in the footer** (Hevy, MacroFactor).
8. **Every page ends at the App Store**, with Apple's badge once a page
   (Apple's "one App Store badge per layout"; the others repeat it).

## What it leaves

- **Ratings, user counts, testimonials, media logos and celebrities.**
  Tropos has none yet, and a made-up one would be a fabricated review.
- **A web sign-up or web purchase.** troposfit.com does not serve the app
  (App1 Q52) and does not sell Pro until the owner chooses how (Q77).
- **Coach bios.** Tropos's plans are its own engine; there are no coaches
  to name.
- **Cookie banners and trackers.** The site loads no analytics and sets no
  cookie, so it needs no banner.
- **A drawn phone around the screens.** Apple's marketing guidelines allow
  only Apple's own bezels and never a drawn copy; the screens stand alone.
