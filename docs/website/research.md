# troposfit.com: what six fitness apps' websites do well

Read on 18 November 2026, from each site's live home page (and MacroFactor's
press kit), before designing troposfit.com (`docs/website/design.md`). Each
note is what the site does, what Tropos takes from it, and what Tropos leaves.
Quotes are the sites' own words as served that day.

## The pattern across all six

1. **One promise, then the platform.** The first screen says what the app is
   for in a few words and, in the line under it, where it runs ("a free
   workout tracker for iOS and Android"). Nobody opens on a feature list.
2. **The way in sits under the promise.** Store badges (Hevy, Runna, Nike
   Run Club), a free-trial button (Runna, Ladder) or a sign-up form (Strava,
   whose product is also a website). Four of the six repeat it at the foot of
   the page.
3. **The app is the picture.** Every site shows real app screens beside
   short headlines, and five of six alternate picture and words down the
   page. MacroFactor uses screens only; Nike, Runna and Ladder add
   photographs of people.
4. **Proof is borrowed when it exists:** ratings counts, press logos and
   reviews. Tropos has none yet and will not invent them.
5. **The footer carries Press, Support, Privacy and Terms** on every site.
   Hevy, Runna, Strava and MacroFactor link a press page.

## Runna ([runna.com](https://www.runna.com/))

- **Does well.** The hero is "Take your running to the next level" with
  "Start Your Free Trial" and "First week free. Cancel anytime." directly
  under the button. Its strongest block shows the product doing a real
  thing: three app screens step through choosing a distance, setting the
  training days and reading a predicted race time ("Run your personal
  best"). A plan finder lays out every goal as a card (5K, 10K, Half
  Marathon, Marathon, Return to Running). The page ends on its opening line
  again.
- **Tropos takes** the product shown working (a planned week, a session, a
  race counted down) rather than adjectives about it; plan cards by goal on
  the Running page; the price and the trial's terms beside the way in on
  the Pro page.
- **Tropos leaves** the three blocks of reviews and ratings (no ratings to
  show yet), the coach biographies and the long FAQ.

## Strava ([strava.com](https://www.strava.com/))

- **Does well.** "Community-Powered Motivation" and "Join for the tracking,
  stay for the community." It sells the people first and the tracker second,
  in three short blocks ("Open, tap, go.", "A no BS network.").
  The subscription section leads with one concrete feature (Beacon, sharing
  your location live) rather than a list.
- **Tropos takes** the order of the Social page (people first, then what you
  do together) and a Pro page that leads with the one thing Pro does that a
  person can picture: a meal photo read for you.
- **Tropos leaves** signing up on the website. troposfit.com points to the
  App Store and does not run the app (App1 Q52).

## Hevy ([hevyapp.com](https://www.hevyapp.com/))

- **Does well.** The H1 is three short verb phrases ("Log Workouts", "Get
  Stronger", "Stay Motivated") and the subline names the platforms and the
  price: "Hevy is a free workout tracker for iOS and Android." Each feature
  block is a headline, two sentences, a short list and a real screen. The
  footer has a Press Center.
- **Tropos takes** the subline that says where the app runs ("Tropos is an
  iPhone app"), the block shape (headline, two sentences, three or four
  lines, one screen) and the footer's columns.
- **Tropos leaves** the user counts, press logos and the Coach product.

## Ladder ([joinladder.com](https://www.joinladder.com/))

- **Does well.** "Know exactly what workout to do, every day" over a phone
  showing the week from Monday to Sunday: the clearest single picture of a
  training plan among the six. "No Credit Card To Start • Cancel anytime."
  sits under every button.
- **Tropos takes** the week as the picture of the plan: Home's session and
  its week of segments are the first app screen on troposfit.com.
- **Tropos leaves** the capitals, the celebrity hero and the quiz before the
  download. Tropos's setup questions are in the app.

## Nike Run Club ([nike.com/gb/nrc-app](https://www.nike.com/gb/nrc-app))

- **Does well.** "Let's run together", one button ("Download the App") and,
  for a visitor on a computer, a QR code: "Scan the code to download the
  app." Each block is an H2 a person would say out loud: "We keep track of
  the details", "Challenge yourself, challenge each other", "Celebrate
  achievements".
- **Tropos takes** the QR code beside the badge on a wide screen (the site
  will often be read on a laptop and the app is iPhone only), and headlines
  in plain spoken sentences.
- **Tropos leaves** the shop's navigation around it.

## MacroFactor ([macrofactor.com](https://macrofactor.com/), [press kit](https://macrofactor.com/press-kit/))

- **Does well.** Dark, screens only, no stock photography. Its tone is the
  closest to Tropos's house voice: its description promises reaching your
  goals "without rigidity", and its case studies are about systems, not
  extreme results. Its press kit is the model for a small company's: an
  about paragraph, a paragraph for each app, the latest product news,
  screenshots in light and dark, four logo files linked one by one, the
  prices and a press address.
- **Tropos takes** the press kit's shape and order, the dark ground and the
  calm tone.
- **Tropos leaves** its home page's missing headline: the first thing on
  macrofactor.com is a product name, not a promise.

## Apple's rules for the badge and for devices

From the [App Store Marketing Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines):

- Apple's badge artwork only, unmodified, not angled or animated; the black
  badge is preferred and its grey border is part of it.
- At least 40 px tall on screen, with clear space of a quarter of its height.
- "Use one App Store badge per layout or video. Place the badge in a
  subordinate position to the image or main message."
- Credit Apple and the Apple logo once, where the site gives its legal
  notices.
- Apple product images only as Apple supplies them ("Use Apple-provided
  product bezels"), never drawn or simulated, and a drawing of a generic
  phone must not carry details unique to Apple's (the sensor housing, the
  side buttons).
- App screens as the app shows them, with "fictional account information
  instead of data from a real person."

What this means for troposfit.com is in `design.md` under "The App Store
badge" and "App screens".
