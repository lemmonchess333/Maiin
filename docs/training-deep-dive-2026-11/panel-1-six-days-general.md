## The rules the target is built from

These are what the builder would be changed to do. Each is a number the owner
can move.

1. One heavy main lift a day: 4 sets at 60 minutes, 3 at 45. The second
   barbell main goes; its place is a compound that needs little warm-up.
2. No lift under 3 sets. A shorter session has fewer lifts, not thinner ones:
   5 lifts at 60 minutes, 4 at 45.
3. Paired days are the same size. A and B differ in the main lift and what
   follows it.
4. Every pull day has a rear-delt lift and a curl. Every push day has a
   side-delt lift and a triceps lift. Every legs day has a squat pattern and a
   hinge.
5. The deadlift is trained once a week at 3 sets of 5 to 8; the other legs day
   takes a Romanian deadlift. Today both legs days hold a squat and a
   deadlift.
6. Rep ranges by place: main 6-10 (deadlift 5-8), second and third lifts 8-12
   or 10-14, small lifts 10-15 or 12-17.

## What to check before saying yes

- **The clock is unchecked.** The 45-minute legs days price at 48 and 49
  minutes on the engine's clock, which also prices the owner's real ten-set
  Legs A at 44 minutes. The target keeps 12 sets and asks for the clock to be
  calibrated against saved sessions before 3 minutes costs a set.
- **Abs come once a week** (Legs B). A second day needs a sixth lift at 60
  minutes or a paired lift at 45.
- **The week is bigger**: 72 sets against 55 at 45 minutes, 95 against 67 at 60. That is in line with the six-day programmes in `market-lifting.md` (17
  to 24 sets a session). It is too much leg work beside a marathon plan;
  decision 16 in the README steps leg work down as run minutes climb, and that
  is a separate print.
- **The engine's own tally would call this over its ceilings**, because it
  counts every indirect set as a whole one (decision 3).
- **This is one setup.** Three, four and five days, a beginner, Build muscle
  and Get stronger each get their own print once this shape is agreed.
