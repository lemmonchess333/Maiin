# Running: what "on par" means, from the market

Researched 11 November 2026. Web sources are numbered in section 6. Tropos claims cite the file read in a checkout of main at `a199cfd7`; anything not read is in section 7.

## 1. Verdict

1. **Plan engine: close to Runna on paper, and more cautious.** Tropos has most of Runna's plan mechanics for 5K to marathon (periodised phases, VDOT paces with consent, goal-pace work, cutbacks, stepped taper, realign, easier week, return-from-layoff). Its defaults are conservative where Runna's are the most aggressive setting, which is exactly where Runna got hurt in the press in 2026.
2. **Delivery is the real gap.** Runna pushes two weeks of structured workouts to Garmin, Apple Watch, COROS, Suunto and Amazfit. Tropos has a phone player only: its Apple Watch companion is built but switched off, phone-led, and there is no Garmin or COROS path at all. A watch-first runner cannot use Tropos today.
3. **Breadth gaps against Runna:** no trail, ultra or 50K, no hills, progression or fartlek sessions, no scheduled holiday or illness mode with a graded return, no B-races, no queued next plan, three guided runs against NRC's hundreds.
4. **Against Strava: not on par, and it is the wrong target.** Strava's strengths are the network, routes and segments. Its own coaching (Instant Workouts, Athlete Intelligence) is weak, and since 10 July 2026 its running plans are Runna's.
5. **Nothing is proven.** Runna has roughly 2 million monthly users, PB stories and a self-reported injury figure. Tropos has a simulator and no runners. "Improves people without injuring them" is a claim neither side can evidence; Tropos cannot make it until real people finish plans on real devices.

## 3. Table stakes

What every serious run-plan app does, in the order a runner notices it missing:

1. **The workout on the wrist.** Structured steps with pace targets on Apple Watch and Garmin at minimum. Runna, Garmin, COROS and Kiprun Pacer (free) all do it [R12][R13][O1][O4]. Tropos: built for Apple Watch but off, phone required; nothing for Garmin.
2. **Runs recorded on a watch count toward the plan.** Universal. Tropos: no, by ADR-0007.
3. **A plan built from a recent time, current volume, days available and a race date.** Tropos has all four.
4. **Per-session pace or effort targets with warm-up, work and cool-down.** Tropos has it.
5. **Move a run, skip a run, and a prompt after a bad week.** Tropos has it.
6. **A way to say "I'm ill" or "I'm away" for a set number of days.** Runna has both, with a graded return [R2][R9]. Tropos has an easier week and a rebuild after three weeks, nothing dated in between.
7. **Pace targets that move as fitness moves.** Runna offers this to every subscriber [R6]. Tropos puts it behind Pro.
8. **A beginner route in.** Tropos has it, free; Runna's went behind the paywall on 5 June 2026 [R24].
9. **Strength alongside the plan.** Now expected: Runna, Garmin and COROS all include it [R11][G1][O1]. Tropos is ahead here.
10. **Race prediction and a post-run verdict.** Tropos has both.

Not table stakes, despite the noise: AI chat, readiness scores, segments. Strava and COROS both shipped connectors to general AI assistants in 2026 instead of building coaches [S6][S8][O8]; Strava's built-in AI is widely dismissed [S10][S4].

## 4. What Runna is known for, and where it is weak

**Known for**

- **Plans that feel personal and are easy to follow.** Coach-written templates fitted by an algorithm to ability, mileage, days and race date [R4][R19]. Reviewers praise the clear session descriptions and interface [R21].
- **The watch experience.** Two weeks of structured sessions on five watch brands, plus its own Apple Watch app with live pace prompts [R12][R13][R25]. Forum users name the Garmin sync and in-run audio as the reason they pay [R28][R29].
- **Life-proofing.** Holiday mode, Not Feeling 100%, realignment, drag-and-drop calendar [R9][R2][R8].
- **Results stories.** PBs in App Store reviews and forums: a 20-minute half-marathon PB, a first sub-hour 10K, "transformed my running" [R28][R29][R17].
- **Scale and momentum.** About 2 million monthly users at the acquisition (reported by the5krunner and a competitor blog, not by Runna's press page) [R17][R34]; Apple App of the Year finalist 2024; 4.9 on the App Store from 76,000 ratings by its own release [R14]. 2026 shipped heat-adjusted paces, queued plans, automatic recovery plans [R14].
- **Strava behind it.** Strava retired its own running plans in favour of Runna on 10 July 2026, shows Runna sessions inside Strava activities, and sells the bundle [S2][S6][S1].

**Known weaknesses**

- **Too aggressive by default.** The Wall Street Journal reported on 9 February 2026 that Runna was adding ways to tone plans down after users reported injuries; secondary coverage says physical therapists described several Runna-related cases a week, naming stress fractures, shin splints and Achilles tendinopathy [R22][R17][R16]. I could not read the WSJ piece itself. The defaults are still the top volume and top difficulty setting [R1].
- **Paces run hot for some.** A year-long user with three 10K PBs called the paces "borderline too fast" and left feeling "wasted" most days [R17]. Reddit users describe very hard speed sessions and warn that the defaults can contribute to injury unless inputs are set low [R28]. One forum user's wife quit because it took the enjoyment out [R29].
- **Volume ramps.** A Women's Health tester felt mileage creep by week three and had to turn the plan down [R21]. A Trustpilot reviewer blames a hip injury on rapid mileage increases and a mileage setting that would not stay corrected; another called the 100K plan's weekly load unsafe [R27].
- **It adapts less than the marketing implies.** Pace changes need a trend and the runner's acceptance, use GPS pace only, and ignore heart rate, terrain, sleep and fuelling [R6]. Runna has not published how the algorithm works [R16]. It does not train to a goal time [R16].
- **Depends on honest inputs.** Several sources put injuries down to runners overstating fitness or returning from a break onto the default progression [R20][R34][R31].
- **No pain log.** The only tool for a niggle is the 14-day adjustment and advice to see a physio [R10].
- **Cross-training does not count.** Only runs sync to the plan; reviewers ask for cycling and swimming credit and for a proper pause [R13][R27].
- **Evidence is thin on both sides.** No peer-reviewed study shows Runna users are injured more than others [R17][R19]. Runna's 43% lower injury figure is self-reported with no cohort given [R18]. The Loughborough review covers exactly the three criticised areas and came five months after the reports [R18].
- **Service and price.** Trustpilot sits at 3.5 to 3.6 from about 1,100 reviews, mostly billing, cancellation and bot support complaints [R27]. That contrasts with the App Store score, and with the 4.9 Trustpilot figure in Runna's own listing [R15]. Beginner plans stopped being free in June 2026 [R24]. The most upvoted Reddit view: not worth it without a race [R28].
- **Friction with Garmin.** No Runna audio through a Garmin; running both devices creates duplicates [R12][R26].

**What this means for Tropos.** Runna's fame is the plan plus the watch. Its exposure is default aggressiveness, and it is spending 2026 retrofitting caution. Tropos already has the cautious defaults; it does not have the watch.

## 5. Where a hybrid app can beat a pure running app

**Evidence that runners want it**

- Over half of Strava's users logged more than one sport in the 2025 Year in Sport data, and weight training grew strongly, most among Gen Z [S12][S13].
- Strength is one of Strava's fastest-growing sport types, with over 400 million uploads in 2025. Strava answered in May 2026 with a strength log, muscle maps and 14 integrations, and quotes an athlete saying hybrid athletes want training connected, not split across apps [S9].
- Garmin's 2025 data shows strength sessions up 29% [S13].
- Hyrox went from 570,000 athletes in 2024/25 to 1.5 million in 2025/26 [O10]. Runna sells Hyrox and functional-fitness plans [R5].
- Runna's own number: runners who add its strength plan are 6% more likely to PB (its data, unverified) [R11].
- Runna reviewers ask for cross-training to count [R27].
- A funded hybrid competitor exists at a much higher price: Edge, from £22.99 a month with human coaches, claiming 20,000 members (its own blog) [O9].

**Where the advantage is real**

1. **Hard days that know about each other.** Runna says strength "complements" the run plan and describes no placement rule or load tracking [R11]. Garmin cannot run a run plan and a cycling plan together [G3]. Tropos schedules both in one week, keeps the long run off lift days where it can, and offers a lighter lift after a hard run (`runPlacement.ts`, `easierToday.ts`).
2. **Progressive lifting, not accessory circuits.** Runna's strength is a running add-on [R11]. Tropos has a full lift engine with a "Support my running" goal.
3. **Fuel tied to the session.** Runna, Strava and NRC have no food engine. Garmin Connect+ comes nearest: nutrition logging since January 2026, with targets that adjust to logged workouts [G6], and it needs a Garmin watch.
4. **Price.** £34.99 a year with run plans free, against £99.99 for Runna and £119.99 for the bundle.

**Where to be honest**

- **Do not sell strength as injury prevention.** A 2024 meta-analysis in Sports Medicine (Wu et al., nine studies, 1,904 runners) found exercise programmes did not significantly cut running injuries overall; only supervised ones did, and most studies carried high bias risk [O11]. The repo's own handoff already says to treat strength as a context-dependent adjunct. The supportable claim is running economy.
- **The window is closing.** Garmin bought TrainingPeaks and TrainHeroic on 22 July 2026 and now holds run coaching, strength coaching, nutrition and readiness in one ecosystem [G7][G6][G2]. Strava has strength logging and Runna. Neither has joined the pieces into one adaptive week yet.
- **Hybrid does not excuse the running gaps.** A hybrid athlete with a Garmin still cannot run a Tropos session from the wrist.
