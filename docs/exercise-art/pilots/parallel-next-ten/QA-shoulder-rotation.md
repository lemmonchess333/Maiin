# Independent QA: Arnold press and Lu raise

Reviewed every distinct native frame against `src/lib/exercises.ts`, the six cues and documented reuse. No image edits. This is still-image/cue review, not actual sequential playback or mobile light/dark approval. Neither set is release approved.

## Arnold press

Three distinct poses, hash-confirmed sequence 1,2,3,3,2,1. The first pose shows dorsal hands toward the viewer and palms toward the athlete at upper chest height. The middle opens the elbows and turns the dumbbells while lifting. The top visibly exposes palm pads toward the viewer with extended arms and two separate dumbbells. Thus the major inward-to-forward grip change is genuinely present; it is not simply an ordinary shoulder press with unchanged grips. The exact intermediate rotation is subtle at this scale, so smoothness remains a playback/readability question. The hold and reverse cues match their reused states, including identical 6→1 start/end.

Confirmed blocker: the top shifts the head, seat/back structure and both shoe contacts upward approximately 25–35 pixels compared with start/middle. This cannot be explained by arm projection, since the bench and feet are fixed. The body/bench scale also changes slightly. Reject continuous-sequence approval until those anchors are repaired. Weights remain separate at top; changing projected dumbbell faces during rotation alone is not a defect. Upper-chest tint becomes lighter through the set, so highlight consistency also needs attention.

## Lu raise

Four distinct poses, hash-confirmed sequence 1,2,3,4,3,2. The set matches the local catalogue variation: arms at sides, partial lateral raise, shoulder-height lateral position, then both arms sweep forward at shoulder height. It does not incorrectly substitute an overhead raise. The return opens the arms laterally before lowering, and frame6's instruction to continue to the sides matches the final 6→1 transition.

Head, torso, pelvis and both shoe footprints appear substantially stable in the inspected stills. Grips stay attached; shoulders remain purple and other muscle regions neutral. The forward dumbbells are close and overlap in projection, but two distinct handles/hands and separate outer heads remain visible. This is not enough evidence to claim physical collision or impossible arm length. Forward-reaching arms are expected to be foreshortened. Main remaining limitation is readability of that forward sweep at mobile size and the abrupt lateral-to-forward timing when using one endpoint frame. No definite major mechanical defect found in these stills; playback/mobile gates remain open.
