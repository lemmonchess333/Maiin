# Independent QA — upright row and landmine press

Reviewed all three distinct native poses for each set and read all six cues. Hash checks confirm frames 4/5/6 exactly reuse 3/2/1 respectively. This is a still-image and cue review, not actual sequential playback or mobile light/dark verification. Neither set receives release approval here.

## Barbell upright row

- Frames 1→2→3 show thigh start, elbow-led ascent, chest-height bar endpoint. Elbows remain approximately at/below shoulder height at the top; no obvious excessive high pull. Top hold and controlled-return cues match reused states. 6→1 is identical resting pose.
- Feet, head and torso location are substantially stable. Bar remains straight with matching modest plates and both hands attached.
- Hand spacing increases modestly from setup to raised poses, despite unchanged camera/bar scale: hand centers roughly x380/640 at start versus x360/655 raised on the 1024-wide image. This is visible grip-position drift, not proof of a grossly impossible lift, but violates fixed grip-width consistency.
- Purple trapezius / lilac shoulder and upper-arm hierarchy is visually consistent. No conspicuous halo. Fine wrist/hand details and motion smoothness still need playback/mobile review.

## Landmine press

- Frames 1→2→3 show bent elbow at shoulder, partial press, then nearly straight extended arm moving up/forward. Torso avoids obvious back lean and both feet retain staggered contacts. Holds and returns match their cue states; 6→1 is identical setup.
- Both hand-to-bar contact and one modest loading plate persist. Full hand at top has very little canvas margin; readability/cropping needs mobile review.
- The floor base appears stationary, but the sleeve-to-pivot connection changes visible location/detail between poses: initial side hinge near the post side becomes a connection nearer the open top. The hinge construction is not sufficiently clear to certify rigid fixed-axis mechanics.
- Projected bar length changes are not by themselves evidence of physical length/radius change; perspective and out-of-plane motion could account for some variation. No calibrated 3D camera model is available, so constant physical radius remains unverified rather than conclusively failed.
- Unworking shoulder/arm is highlighted alongside working side, reducing unilateral activation clarity. Catalogue muscle hierarchy is otherwise broadly retained. No actual motion review performed.
