# Independent QA: Meadows and Pendlay rows

Inspected all four distinct Meadows frames, the sole retained Pendlay frame, both rejected Pendlay intermediates, catalogue instructions and cues. Still-image review only; no playback/mobile verification and no release approval.

## Meadows row

Six files, hash-confirmed pose order 1,2,3,4,3,2. The staggered stance, free hand braced against the forward thigh, steady hip hinge and overhand grip on the loaded sleeve are visible. Both feet, head, support hand and landmine base remain substantially stable. The elbow travels back and the loaded end reaches the hip/lower-rib area, consistent with the catalogue. The reverse cues match reused poses; frame6 is near the stretch rather than the final fully extended position, with completion at 6→1.

Equipment remains an unresolved gate: shaft segment markings and collar appearance change. The projected pivot-to-loaded-end distance increases markedly as the bar rises. Projection can change during rotation, so this observation alone does not prove a stretching bar; however, the set provides insufficient geometric evidence to establish one fixed rigid radius, especially alongside changing shaft markings. Keep it a draft pending equipment/sequence verification. The narrow rear-three-quarter view also makes precise perpendicular stance relative to the bar harder to judge, although the depicted side-on relation is broadly plausible. No obvious support-hand detachment or fixed-foot jump was found.

## Pendlay row

Incomplete: only frame1 exists. Six cues are explicitly planned with availability flags; do not count this as a converted six-frame exercise. The starting frame shows straight arms, a flat near-horizontal torso, knees flexed, and plates positioned as a floor-supported bar over the feet. Black background lacks an explicit ground plane, so exact simultaneous plate/floor contact cannot be independently established from the still. Oblique camera also limits precise torso angle measurement. It is nevertheless a credible starting candidate, not proof of the dead-stop cycle.

Both rejected intermediate images retain broad body position but spread the working grip relative to the same bar segment compared with frame1. The correction does not restore the original grip spacing; bar/plate perspective also changes. This supports rejecting the intermediates and stopping rather than fabricating a return sequence. No lower-chest endpoint, controlled descent, or actual floor reset has been demonstrated. The dead-stop requirement remains unverified.
