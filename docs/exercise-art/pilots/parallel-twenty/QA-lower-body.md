## Findings

### bodyweight-lunge

Standing-to-stride translation is intended. Rear heel may lift and rear shoe/toe geometry changes during a forward step; I do not treat the recorded initial ~50px rear-toe movement alone as a hard anchor failure. Once the stride is planted, foot contact locations should settle. Frames2–4 broadly retain the planted front sole and rear toe while the knee bends. Main technique defect: the rear knee remains substantially more open than the catalogue's near-90-degree bottom, producing a long, stretched lunge with the rear knee well behind the hip. Frames3 and4 are close in front-thigh angle but differ in rear-knee bend, so phase labels must distinguish the actual movement. Hands stay on hips, torso stays upright, no wrong equipment. Initial floor/scale consistency remains uncertain without sequential review; no blanket fixed-feet rule applies across the forward step.

### barbell-step-ups

Moving body, bar and trailing shoe upward is intended. Lead shoe should remain planted while the leg extends; box must remain fixed. Frames1–3 show a plausible drive with the rear foot airborne and bar retained on upper traps. Frame4 appropriately brings both feet onto the box, but the box's upper plane rises relative to its stable base (about17px by visual comparison), changing the box height. This is a true rigid-equipment failure, not athlete perspective. The lead shoe also shifts toward the far/right side; a deliberate foot reposition might be possible but is not shown as a step, so continuity remains unverified. Bar/plate contact and modest single plates are broadly coherent. Fix box first and re-check planted lead-foot identity.

### front-squat

Front-rack variation is recognisable: high forward elbows, bar in front of neck, upright torso and squatting hips/knees. Upper-body descent is expected; shoe movement is not. Both feet rise in the deeper frames relative to the same canvas and initial floor reference, corroborating author's ~20–35px anchor finding. This warrants repair. Frames3 and4 depict nearly the same depth, with front thigh projection changing little; not enough evidence to call four evenly spaced poses. Front-rack fingertip contact and near elbow support still deserve close inspection. Single plate per side and overall bar remain broadly consistent. No claim that foreshortened thighs prove changing limb length.

### single-leg-calf-raise

Partial set correctly contains only numeric frames1 and2; third endpoint is explicitly rejected. Intended motion is heel/body rising about a forefoot pivot, with step and rail stationary. Frames1–2 show some heel lift and largely fixed equipment. Rejected3 visibly shifts the whole step and rail upward relative to original canvas, so this is a true equipment-anchor failure. Head/body rise is expected and is not itself a drift defect; however combined equipment translation prevents reliable assessment of actual ankle excursion. Original frame1 shows heel at approximately step height rather than a clear below-step stretch; future full sequence must visibly include the catalogue's controlled heel drop. Do not pad partial with copied frames or count it complete.
