# Independent upper-body bodyweight artwork review

Static native-image review against exact catalogue definitions. This report does not approve runtime playback, mobile light/dark display or biomechanics. No source assets changed.

## Diamond push-ups

Inspected unique frames 1–4. Touching thumbs/index fingers form a clear diamond, hands are beneath the chest, elbows generally track back and the body stays broadly in a plank. Purple triceps and lighter chest/deltoids follow catalogue hierarchy. Canonical identity and white trainers consistent.

Bottom pose shows small changes in hand outlines and shoe positions; these ground contacts require playback inspection. No gross hand detachment or torso sag established by static review. The four-pose sequence is meaningful; frames 5 and 6 reuse 3 and 2 on return, so frame 6 is still pressing, with extension completed by 6→1. Its cue says continue to straight arms, which is compatible. Do not describe frame 6 as already at full extension.

Recommendation: candidate suitable for the next playback/contact-anchor review, not release-approved here.

## Pike push-up

Inspected unique frames 1–4. Inverted-V posture and elbow-flexion progression are readable, with the hips remaining high; shoulder highlighting is coherent. However the torso remains markedly diagonal rather than nearly vertical above the hands as the catalogue explicitly specifies. At bottom, the head is depicted approaching the hand region with a sideways/face-facing orientation; the crown-to-floor path is not convincingly shown. This is a meaningful instruction/image mismatch, not merely a contour difference.

Hands also move slightly across descent frames, and these are fixed load-bearing contacts. Keep this set unapproved until the torso/head mechanics and hand anchors are corrected or independently confirmed. Frames 5/6 are return poses 3/2, with full extension reached at 6→1.

## Inverted row

Inspected unique frames 1–4. Overhand grip, heel support and straight-arm start are recognisable. Bar and rack anchors remain broadly fixed. The endpoint brings the bar across the clavicle/lower-neck region, rather than clearly contacting the chest below it; this needs correction before release. The neck/head also extend back noticeably at the endpoint.

Apparent shoulder-to-heel span grows across the ascent. Some projected length change can occur when a rigid body rotates in perspective, so pixel distance alone is insufficient to prove anatomical stretching. Nevertheless the apparent torso/leg proportions change enough that a fixed-length heel-centred arc should be checked in playback; this remains an unresolved concern, not a measured physical failure. Identity and equipment remain visually coherent; no release approval.

## Pull-ups

Inspected unique frames 1–4. Bar height, rack and hand contacts remain broadly fixed, the athlete rises without an obvious kick, and the profile chin is clearly above the bar in frame 4. Rear-view hand backs are consistent with an overhand grip, although exact thumb wrapping is not fully clear at this scale. The static head turn right is consistent across frames rather than a new turn during ascent.

The start labelled dead hang retains visible elbow bend, so it does not clearly teach full arm extension. Frame 2 already shows appreciable elbow flexion while its cue describes setting the shoulder blades; catalogue specifically puts this before the arms start moving. This is a meaningful phase/cue mismatch. Correct the full-hang pose and either illustrate a genuine scapular initiation phase or align its cue with the actual early pull before release.

Small torso-length/contour differences remain a playback concern; overall translation upward is expected and is not itself anchor drift. Feet are free-hanging, not fixed ground contacts. Frames 5/6 reuse 3/2, so full return occurs at 6→1; as long as frame 1 is corrected, that mapping is mechanically appropriate.

## Scope and conclusion

All four sets now have independent static inspection. Diamond is the strongest candidate for sequential playback review. Pike, inverted row and pull-ups retain the meaningful technique/phase concerns above. No set is promoted to production-approved by this report. Equipment projection or free-body translation alone was not treated as a failure. Native image edits, application playback, light/dark checks and source-registry changes were not performed.
