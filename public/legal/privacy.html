Who we are

Tropos is operated by Myles Kennedy, trading as Tropos, who is the data controller for the personal data described in this policy. For any question about your data, or to exercise any of the rights in sections 5 and 6, contact support@troposfit.com.

1. Information We Collect

Tropos collects the following information when you create an account and use the app:

  • Account Information: your email address, your display name and how you sign in (email and password, Google or Apple). If you sign in with Apple, Apple may give us a private relay address instead of your own. If you sign in with Google or Apple, the name on that account is your display name until you change it, and with Google, the photo on your Google account is your profile photo until you change it.
  • Profile and Fitness Details: your sex for the calorie calculation (if you give it), year of birth (an older account may hold an age or an age range instead), height and weight, activity level and training experience, any injury limits you tell us about, your maximum heart rate, your goals (including a goal weight), and the details of any race you are training for, such as its distance, date and event. Sex is optional: choose Prefer not to say in setup, or Not set on your profile in Settings, and Tropos stores none and uses a figure halfway between the male and female ones. We also keep when you agreed to Tropos storing your health details, and to which wording of that question.
  • Setup Before You Have an Account: you answer setup’s questions before you make an account. Until you do, your answers, including your health details once you have agreed to Tropos storing them, are kept only on your phone or in your browser, and none of them is sent to our servers. They are kept for 2 days from your last answer and deleted the next time Tropos opens after that, and deleted at once when you sign out or when an account other than the one you make from Save your plan signs in. When you make your account from Save your plan, they become your plan. If you agreed to Tropos storing your health details before you had an account, that agreement lapses after 2 days, after 2 hours with Tropos in the background, or when the app is closed, and setup asks again.
  • Activity Data: workouts, sets and personal records, runs, meals, water, weight logs over time, your notes, and the training plan you follow. Also the routes you save to follow again (a route you import, plan or keep from a run), and the running shoes you add with the distance run in each.
  • Location During Runs: while a run is recording, your GPS position, including when the app is in the background or the screen is locked, so the route, distance and pace are complete. A route is recorded only while a run is recording. At other times the app uses your position only for something you ask for: an approximate position (to about a kilometre) for the weather before a run, the start of a route you plan, or the place of a privacy zone you add. GPS points that fall inside a privacy zone (for example around your home or workplace) are removed from a route before it is saved or shared.
  • Motion During Runs (iPhone): while a run with auto-pause on is recording, your phone’s motion and step data, to pause the clock when you stop and start it again as you set off. It is read on your phone and is not stored or sent to Tropos.
  • Nutrition Data: food logs, including foods you add from a barcode scan or a search and the barcode of a food you scan, and calorie and macro tracking data. The words you type in a food search go straight from your phone to Open Food Facts, and Tropos keeps none of your searches (section 7).
  • Apple Health (optional): if you connect it, your daily step count, which Pro compares with your activity level. With Pro, if you add them, your sleep, resting heart rate and heart rate variability for readiness. When available, Apple Watch runs also read heart rate during a run, and saving a completed Tropos run as a workout in Apple Health is a separate choice. See section 8.
  • Heart rate sensor (optional): if you pair a Bluetooth chest strap or armband, Tropos reads your heart rate from it during a run. See section 8.
  • Progress Photos (optional): if you add progress photos, new ones are encrypted on your device with AES-GCM before they are uploaded. Some older photos were stored in an earlier format without that encryption. See section 3 for what the encryption does and does not mean. If you share one, the image is made on your device and goes only where you send it from your phone’s share sheet; Tropos does not post it anywhere.
  • Meal Photos (optional): if you use the camera to log a meal, and you have allowed AI food analysis, the photo is sent to Google for analysis and then kept only on the device that took it. We do not store it on our servers. It is deleted automatically after 90 days. See section 7 for the detail.
  • Profile, Post and Space Photos (optional): the profile photo you set, photos you add to a session you share, and photos you attach to a Space post. Before a photo for a post is uploaded, the app saves it again at a smaller size, which leaves out the location and other details your camera stored in the file.
  • Run Map Images (optional): when you share a run with a route, the app draws a picture of the map of the route it shares (with the start and finish hidden unless you turned that off, and your privacy zones removed) and stores it with the post. It is checked as photos are (section 7) and deleted with the post.
  • Segments (optional): a segment you make from a stretch of one of your runs (its line, name and length), and the times our server records when a run of yours passes along a segment, yours or someone else’s. See section 4.
  • Shared Cards (optional): when you share a card of a session, a day’s food or a week from the share sheet, a copy of the card’s figures and words as the card shows them, without any you hid, so that its link can open it. It never holds a route, a coordinate, the photo the card was drawn on or your name. See section 4.
  • Social Activity (optional): the posts you share, comments, props, follows, Circle check-ins and meet-ups, Space posts, and the reports you make about content or accounts. Also the posts you hide from your feed, the people you block, the challenges you join with your progress and place in them, the medals they award, your weekly league standing if you join the league, a race you choose to show on your profile, and who invited you, if you joined from someone’s invite.
  • Notifications in the app: the notices in your tray, such as “Sam gave you props”. One about something a person did carries their name, so when you give props, comment, follow or join from an invite, your name goes into the other person’s tray. Each notice is deleted after 90 days, and when either account is deleted.
  • Subscription Records: whether you have Pro, where you bought it, its product and dates, and the store’s transaction identifiers. If you start a free trial, its dates and the price you were shown, so we can remind you before it turns into a paid subscription.
  • Usage Data and Analytics Identifiers: which screens and features you use, and events such as finishing a workout, a run or a scan, from the health-details agreement in setup on, tied to an identifier for this installation of the app, not to your account. They carry no figure about your body, food or training. Your account also keeps when you saved your first session, when you trained in your fourth week, and when your first trial and first paid period began, and which of these your phone has already sent to analytics, so each is counted once. See section 2.
  • Notification Token (optional): if you allow notifications, the token your phone or browser gives Tropos so that notifications reach that device, and whether it is an iPhone or a browser. Signing out on the device removes it from your account.
  • Crash and Error Reports: error messages and where in the app they happened, with the address of the screen (any name or id in it taken out) and your device’s browser or app details, and in the iPhone app, its crashes.
  • Preferences and Time Zone: units, theme, notification and sharing choices, and your device’s time zone, so daily limits and reminders follow your local day. For each email you can choose (section 2), whether you said yes or no, when, where and to which wording. For the evening streak reminder and the Monday review, when each was last sent and whether you opened Tropos after it.

2. How We Use Your Data

We use your data to:

  • Run the app: your dashboard, plan, logs and progress
  • Calculate performance figures, streaks and badges
  • Sync your data across your devices
  • Show what you choose to share to other people in the app
  • Estimate what is in a meal photo or a typed meal, if you allow it (section 7)
  • Send account emails, such as a password reset
  • If you choose them, email you your weekly review on Mondays, and one email at 2 weeks and one at a month if you stop logging (we ask in setup, off until you turn it on)
  • Send the reminders and notifications you turn on. Our server sends at most one notification a day, and 5 in any 7 days, that is not about something a person did (such as the evening streak reminder, the Monday review, a challenge or a badge). A reminder you leave unanswered 3 times running (you did not open Tropos that day, tap it or do what it asked) is turned off, and Home tells you once. You can turn it back on in Settings > Notifications.
  • Understand how the app is used and fix what breaks (analytics, and crash and error reports)

Analytics. Google Analytics for Firebase receives the usage events described in section 1: which screens you open, what you do there (such as finishing a workout or tapping a chart), the choices you make in settings and in your plan, and timings, such as how long a screen took to appear or logging a meal took. It receives the choices you made in setup: your goal (such as building muscle or losing fat), how many days a week you train, whether you lift, run or both, and whether your runs follow a race plan, a starter plan or neither. Events from your food diary carry counts, such as how many rows a day shows, never what is in them. It receives no figure about your body, food, sleep, heart or training, such as a weight, calories, a distance, a pace or a score. Google also works out an approximate location, such as the country or city, from your connection. Analytics never receives your email address, your name, GPS routes, what you type about meals, the names of your foods, or your notes.

It also receives a few events about your first days, each sent once: your account made and how (Apple, Google or email), setup finished, and the trial offer seen. And once for each account, with how many days after setup each came: your first saved session and whether it was a lift or a run, a session in your fourth week, and your first trial and first paid period, with whether each was bought in the app or on the web. None of them carries your name, your email address or a figure about you.

When analytics starts. Google Analytics, in the iPhone app and on the web, counts how the app is used so we can improve it. It starts at the health-details agreement in setup, which says so, or when you sign in to an account that has agreed. Nothing is sent to Google before that screen, and after you sign out nothing is sent until you reach it again or sign in. Before it, our own server counts only how many times a day the welcome screen is shown and setup is started, and how many accounts are made: totals with no identifier, sent without reading anything from your phone, which cannot be linked to you and are deleted after about 13 months. Google’s data sharing settings, Google signals and ad personalisation are off for Tropos, so Google uses this data only to give us the analytics.

Turning analytics off. Usage analytics is on from there until you turn it off, with Share usage analytics in Settings > Social & privacy. Off, the phone or browser you are using stops sending anything to Google Analytics at once, and your account keeps the choice so your other devices follow it once you sign in on them. Crash and error reports are not analytics and do not follow this switch.

We do not use your data for advertising, Tropos shows no adverts, and we do not sell your data.

3. Data Storage & Security

Your data is stored securely using Google Firebase with industry-standard encryption. Data is transmitted over HTTPS and stored in encrypted databases. We use Firebase Authentication for secure user authentication.

New progress photos are additionally encrypted on your device (AES-GCM-256) before upload, so they are not stored as plain image files. Anyone browsing raw storage sees ciphertext rather than pictures.

To be precise about what that protects: this is not end-to-end encryption. New photos use a random key for each photo, kept in private account metadata separately from the image storage. This lets you access them after signing in on another device. Tropos can access these keys and is technically able to decrypt the photos. Older uploads can use a key derived from your account identifier or an unencrypted legacy format. Those photos do not gain the new protection unless they are uploaded again.

4. Data Sharing

We do not sell or rent your personal data.

What other people see. Your display name, profile photo, streaks, badges and medals are visible to other signed-in Tropos users, and so is a race you choose to show on your profile. Anyone signed in can find you by searching for your name. Your profile also holds whether you set up Tropos to lift, run or both, when your account was made and the last day your streak counted. The app does not show those three to other people, but anyone signed in can read them. Your logs (meals, weight, workouts and runs) are private to your account unless you share them. Comments on a session you share are seen by whoever can see that session. Circle check-ins are seen by the people in that Circle, and a Circle meet-up (what it is, when, the place, note and pace groups you type, and who is going) by the Circle’s members. Spaces are open to everyone signed in: anyone signed in to Tropos can read a Space’s posts, the comments and props on them, and who has joined it (each member’s name and photo), whether or not they have joined it themselves. If you join a challenge, your name, photo and progress in it are visible to other signed-in Tropos users. If you join the weekly league, the people in your group (about 20) see your first name, your photo and your week: the sessions you did against those your plan had, and the points they give. Leaving the league removes your standing at once.

Sharing sessions. When you share a workout or a run, you choose its audience: public, followers only, or private. You can also choose, in Settings > Social & privacy > Sharing, to share runs or workouts automatically with your followers or publicly; each session of that kind you finish is then posted without asking. Until you choose, you are asked each time. A shared route has its start and end hidden unless you turn that off, and your privacy zones are removed from it.

Public posts on the web. Anyone with the link to a session you shared publicly can open it on troposfit.com, signed in or not. The page asks search engines not to list it. That page shows your display name and profile photo, the session’s name and date, its figures (a run’s distance, time, pace and climb, or a workout’s time, volume, number of exercises and any record) and, for a run, a map of its route with the start and finish cut off and your privacy zones removed. A followers-only or private post never opens there.

Shared cards. A card you share from the share sheet that is not of a public post can end with a link to its own page on troposfit.com. Anyone with the link can open it, signed in or not, and the page asks search engines not to list it. It shows the card’s figures and words as you shared them (such as a run’s distance, time and pace, a workout’s volume, or a day’s calories and macros), never a route, a coordinate, the photo the card was drawn on or your full name. The line above the card names you by your first name, and while the invite code you had when you shared it is still yours, the page offers it to join Tropos. A card of a post or a session stops opening when you delete that post or session, and its copy is deleted. A card of a day’s food or a week opens for 30 days and is then deleted. Deleting your account deletes them all.

Spaces you join. The sessions you share publicly are shown in each Space you have joined, including up to 20 from the 28 days before you joined. Each week your sessions also count towards the Space’s totals (how many members trained, their sessions and distance), which name no one and are shown only once at least 5 members trained that week.

Segments. A segment is a stretch of one of your runs that you name. Anyone signed in can open its line, name and length, and it does not name you. Each outdoor GPS run saved after a segment is made is checked against it on our server, so your runs are timed on segments other people made, and theirs on yours. When you make a segment, your own last 50 runs are checked against it too. Each time is kept in the runner’s own account, where only they see it. Your best time on a segment is shown to your accepted followers, and only when it comes from a run you shared with followers or publicly and neither its start nor its end is in one of your privacy zones. Anyone else can report a segment, and Tropos can remove it. Deleting a run, a segment or your account deletes the times that go with it.

Followers. Someone becomes your follower once you accept their request, unless you turn off Approve followers in Settings > Social & privacy, when anyone can follow you. A request is seen only by you and the person who asked. Who you follow and who follows you are visible to other signed-in Tropos users.

Some data goes to the services listed in section 7, each for the purpose stated there.

7. Third-Party Services

These services receive personal data to provide their part of Tropos:

  • Google (Firebase): Firebase Authentication (sign-in), Firebase Hosting (which serves troposfit.com, including the pages of public posts and shared cards), Cloud Firestore (your data), Cloud Storage (progress, profile, post and Space photos, and the map images of runs you share), Cloud Functions (our server code), App Check (which checks that requests come from the real app; on the web it uses reCAPTCHA), Firebase Cloud Messaging (which delivers notifications to your phone or browser), Google Analytics for Firebase (usage analytics, section 2) and, in the iPhone app, Firebase Crashlytics (crash reports, below).
  • Photo checks (Google Cloud Vision): the photo you set as your profile photo, the photos you add to a session you share or attach to a Space post, and the map image of a run you share are checked by Google Cloud Vision’s SafeSearch before other people can see them, and one with nudity is deleted. Google processes the photo only to return the check and does not use it to train its models.
  • AI Food Analysis (Google Gemini): if you allow it, the photo you take of a meal or a nutrition label, or a meal you type (Pro), is sent to Google’s Gemini on Vertex AI to estimate what is in it and its macros. Tropos asks before the first one is sent, and you can turn it off at any time in Settings > Social & privacy. Google processes it to return the estimate and may hold it briefly under its terms for this service, for example to detect abuse. Google does not use it to train its models. Tropos does not keep meal photos on its servers. The photo stays on your phone and is sent to Google’s Gemini only to be read. After analysis, it is kept only on the device that took it, so your meal diary can show it. It is not backed up to iCloud, does not sync between your devices, and is deleted automatically after 90 days, the furthest back the diary can be viewed. Deleting the app, or getting a new phone, removes these photos; your logged meals and their nutrition data are unaffected. Barcode scans do not use AI: the barcode is read on your phone and looked up in Open Food Facts (below).
  • Apple: Sign in with Apple (if you use it), Apple Health (optional steps, sleep and heart for readiness and, when available, run heart rate and workout export; section 8), In-App Purchase (Pro bought in the app; Apple handles the payment), the Apple Push Notification service (notifications on your iPhone, through Firebase Cloud Messaging) and App Attest (which confirms that requests come from the real app on your iPhone).
  • RevenueCat: manages Pro subscriptions, bought in the app or on the web, and runs the checkout on the web (RevenueCat Billing). It holds your Tropos account ID and your purchase history, and for a purchase on the web, the email address you pay with. We receive from it whether you have Pro, its product and dates, and whether it is a trial.
  • Stripe: takes the payment for Pro bought on the web, through RevenueCat’s checkout. Stripe receives your card details and the email address you pay with. Tropos never receives your card details.
  • Resend: sends account emails, such as verifying your email address or resetting your password, the reminder before a free trial turns into a paid subscription, and, if you choose them, your weekly review and the emails after a break. It receives your email address and the email. It also carries the alert Tropos gets when someone makes a report: the reason, what kind of thing was reported and any note the person reporting wrote, which can quote what they reported.
  • Open Food Facts: barcode lookups and food searches. The barcode or the words you search for go straight from your phone, with its IP address; no account details are sent, and Tropos keeps none of your searches.
  • OpenFreeMap: the maps. It receives the area of the map being shown and your IP address.
  • MET Norway (weather): the weather before a run. Our server asks for the forecast at your approximate location (to about a kilometre); MET Norway never sees your phone or your account.
  • Route Planning (Mapbox, earlier builds): Road-aware planning is no longer offered. In earlier builds, the map points you tapped (or, for a generated loop, your chosen start point plus a handful of nearby points we compute from it and your chosen distance) were sent from our server to Mapbox’s Directions service to calculate a walking route. We sent only those coordinates (never your name, account details, saved routes, or run history) and did not separately log the request coordinates. When you chose Save & follow, the returned route geometry was saved privately to your account.
  • GitHub: the exercise demonstration images come from a public library hosted on GitHub, which receives your IP address only.

These services have their own privacy policies governing data handling.

Where your data goes. Tropos is run from the UK, and your data is also processed in the United States: our server code runs in Google Cloud’s us-central1 region, Google’s Gemini on Vertex AI analyses meals in the US, and RevenueCat, Resend and Stripe are US companies. Google, Resend and Stripe are certified under the UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework (the UK-US data bridge). For RevenueCat, the safeguard is the UK’s International Data Transfer Addendum to the EU standard contractual clauses, in its data processing terms. Write to support@troposfit.com for a copy of the safeguard that covers a transfer.

Error Reporting: We automatically collect crash reports and error logs to improve app stability. They are stored with your account, and in the iPhone app Google’s Firebase Crashlytics also receives them, with your account’s ID but not your name or email address. Reports include error messages and stack traces, the address of the screen the app was on with any name or id in it taken out (an exercise’s page is kept as /history/exercise/:id, never with the exercise’s name), and your device’s browser or app details (its user agent), but do not contain sensitive personal data such as passwords, meal content, or workout details.

8. Health & Fitness Data

Tropos keeps the health and fitness data you log, including body weight, nutrition, workouts and GPS running data, to provide your fitness tracking. It is never used for advertising and never sold. If you choose them, your weekly review email carries your week’s sessions and food days, and the emails after a break go when you have not logged anything for 2 weeks and for a month (section 2). None of it is sent to Google Analytics, which receives no figure about your body, food or training and never your name (section 2).

Apple Health. If you connect steps, Tropos reads your daily step count on your phone. With Pro, it compares last week’s average with the activity level your calorie target assumes, and offers to change the level when they differ; nothing changes unless you accept, and then only the new activity level is saved. The step count is not stored or sent to Tropos. With Pro, if you add sleep and heart, Tropos reads your sleep, resting heart rate and heart rate variability for the last four weeks to say how ready you are for today’s session. They are read on your phone each time and are not stored or sent to Tropos. Apple Watch runs and workout export are not yet in the App Store version of Tropos; this is how they work when they are available. If you use Apple Watch runs, Tropos reads heart rate during that run to show a live reading and calculate a run summary. The Watch reads it through a workout session, and Apple asks an app that runs one for permission to save workouts as well; the Watch saves none. Raw heart rate samples are held on your device until you save or discard the run; a saved run can include average and maximum heart rate and time in heart rate zones. Those values are not used to change calorie targets or sent to product analytics. If you separately turn on workout export, Tropos writes a completed run to Apple Health after it is saved to your account. The exported workout contains its time, distance and estimated energy, without a GPS route. Tropos does not import Apple Health workouts into your training plan. You can change either choice in Tropos settings and remove Apple Health access in the Health app.

Heart rate sensor. If you pair a Bluetooth heart rate sensor, such as a chest strap or an armband, Tropos uses Bluetooth to read your heart rate from it during a run, to show a live reading, to tell you when an easy run goes above your easy zone, and to calculate a run summary. Raw readings are held on your device until you save or discard the run, and the sensor’s name is kept on this iPhone to reconnect to it. A saved run can include average and maximum heart rate and time in heart rate zones, as with Apple Watch runs, and those values are not used to change calorie targets or sent to product analytics. You can forget the sensor in Settings > Run plan.
